💡 Idiom Mastery: Complete Guide & Explanations

👥 Visitors: 0

Idioms are unique expressions whose meanings cannot be understood by translating word-for-word. Learning idioms means learning how native speakers visualize a situation. Here is an in-depth guide to essential English idioms.

Part 1: Achievement & Hard Work

  • Hit the books
    Meaning: To begin studying hard / To study with high focus.
    Detailed Explanation: The word "hit" here is a metaphor for "fully focusing on." This idiom describes a strong intention to master the material, usually used when an exam is approaching.
    "I have a big exam tomorrow, so I need to hit the books tonight."
  • Pass with flying colors
    Meaning: To pass something with a very high score / To succeed brilliantly.
    Detailed Explanation: Originally from warships returning home after a victory while flying all their flags (colors). It means you achieved a result far above average.
    "She studied for months and passed her exam with flying colors."
  • Go the extra mile
    Meaning: To put in extra effort / To do more than what is expected.
    Detailed Explanation: Describes someone who does more than what is required to achieve a perfect result. It shows high dedication.
    "Our teacher always goes the extra mile to help us."

Part 2: Communication & Social Interaction

  • Spill the beans
    Meaning: To reveal a secret / To let the information out.
    Detailed Explanation: Used when someone intentionally or accidentally reveals confidential information that was supposed to be hidden.
    "Don't tell Mark; he always spills the beans."
  • Beat around the bush
    Meaning: To avoid getting to the point / To speak in a roundabout way.
    Detailed Explanation: Used for people who are hesitant to speak honestly or afraid to deliver the main point, causing them to talk about unnecessary things instead.
    "Stop beating around the bush and tell me the truth."
  • Take it with a grain of salt
    Meaning: To not believe something completely / To be skeptical.
    Detailed Explanation: Suggests that we should doubt certain information because the source might be inaccurate or prone to exaggeration.
    "He exaggerates, so take his stories with a grain of salt."

Part 3: Emotions & Health

  • On thin ice
    Meaning: In a dangerous position / At risk of getting into trouble.
    Detailed Explanation: A situation where someone has already made many mistakes, and one more small mistake will result in severe punishment.
    "After failing two quizzes, I'm on thin ice."
  • Under the weather
    Meaning: Feeling unwell / Slightly sick.
    Detailed Explanation: A polite way to say you feel a bit sick (headache, flu, fatigue) without having to explain the details of your illness.
    "I’m feeling under the weather, so I’ll stay home."
💡 Study Tip:

Do not try to translate idioms word-for-word because the meaning will become strange. Focus on the context of the situation and the mental image behind the explanation to make your speech sound more natural.
